# Simulation of a Ransomware Attack

Bachelor's thesis by **Lorenzo Giandomenico**, University of Calabria, Bachelor's Degree in Computer Science, academic year **2024/2025**.

Supervisor: **Prof. Giovambattista Ianni**.

> [!WARNING]
> The thesis documents techniques that can compromise systems and cause irreversible data loss. It describes a ransomware attack simulation carried out in a controlled virtual laboratory for educational purposes. Commands, scripts and configurations shown in the documents can be destructive if executed.

## Academic context

The work examines a simulated ransomware attack in a virtual environment involving Windows 10, Windows Server 2019 with Active Directory, and Kali Linux. It discusses phishing, privilege escalation, lateral movement and file encryption, with the aim of understanding the associated security risks and the importance of security awareness.

### Fictional scenario and laboratory data

The company named **Sistema**, the employee identities, the profiles and contact details presented in the attack scenario are fictional and were created for the simulation. The LinkedIn and search-result examples are reconstructions of the fictional scenario, not evidence of an attack against an actual company or person.

All attack operations were performed exclusively on virtual machines owned by the author or used with the owner's explicit authorization, within the controlled laboratory. No third-party production system was targeted as part of the simulation.

The passwords, authentication hashes and keys shown in the documents belong exclusively to the laboratory. They have not been reused on real accounts or production systems.

Public domain and provider names appearing in the examples, including `sistema.com`, `sistema.ru` and Outlook, are illustrative naming choices. Their use does not imply any affiliation with their actual owners, ownership of the listed email addresses, or involvement of those owners in the simulation. Statements about SPF, DMARC, vulnerabilities or security configurations describe the simulated scenario and are not assessments of the security of actual organizations or services.
